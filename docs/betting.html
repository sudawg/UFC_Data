Would naive betting have paid off?

Flat $100 stake on every fight in view, always taking the favorite in one line and always taking the underdog in the other. Draws and no-contests are treated as a push (stake returned, $0 profit). This is a backtest against closing-line prices, not a trading recommendation.

About this data. Fight results come from Data/fight_data.csv (8,679 UFC fights, 1994–2026); betting lines come from Data/moneyline.csv, matched by fighter names and event date. Caveats:
  • Moneyline coverage only spans – — fights outside that window can't be matched regardless of quality.
  • Within that window, – of fights matched to a moneyline record; the rest had no scraped line, an unresolvable fighter-name mismatch, or fell on an event the odds source didn't cover.
  • The odds source contains duplicate scrapes for some fights (open vs. close line, or repeat listings). Duplicates were averaged; a small number (~2%) of averaged pairs produced impossible American odds (between −100 and +100) and were dropped rather than guessed at.
  • ROI figures use a flat, unrealistic $100-per-fight strategy with no bankroll management, line shopping, or vig comparison across books — they illustrate market efficiency, not a viable betting system.
